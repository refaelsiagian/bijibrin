SECTION 4: MULTI-TRAIT SEED ARCHETYPES (PRACTICAL IDENTIFICATION GROUPS)
==========================================================================================
Combining color, shape, and size into 8 intuitive visual archetypes:

ARCHETYPE 1: THE CORAL & SAGA BEADS (Vivid Red / Bicolor, Round/Plump)
  Concept: Eye-catching decorative/jewelry seeds; hard, glossy, scarlet or red-and-black; non-flat.
  Species:
    - Abrus precatorius (2/3 red, 1/3 black eye; round)
    - Adenanthera pavonina (Uniform scarlet red; biconvex lens)

SECTION 5: HIGH-SIMILARITY / LOOKALIKE PAIRS & HOW TO DISTINGUISH THEM
==========================================================================================

* Adenanthera pavonina vs. Adenanthera microsperma:
  Diagnostic: Both are brilliant scarlet red beads. A. pavonina is larger (8-10 mm), symmetrical biconvex lens-shaped. A. microsperma is smaller (6-8 mm) and slightly heart-shaped (cordate).

* Abrus precatorius vs. Erythrina abyssinica:
  Diagnostic: Both have brilliant scarlet red and black. Abrus has a true 2/3 red, 1/3 solid black cap around the hilum, and is smaller (5-7 mm). Erythrina is entirely red except for a narrow black hilum scar, and is much larger (9-12 mm).

* Caesalpinia bonduc vs. Sophora tomentosa:
  Diagnostic: Both are perfectly round, hard spherical marbles. C. bonduc is stony porcelain-grey/ash and very large (15-22 mm). S. tomentosa is pale tan/buff-yellow and medium-sized (6-8 mm).

* Enterolobium cyclocarpum vs. Samanea saman:
  Diagnostic: Both are brown seeds with a contrasting yellowish-orange pleurogram ring. Enterolobium is massive (16-22 mm), very thick and rounded. Samanea is medium-sized (8-11 mm) and more slender/oblong.

* Acacia auriculiformis vs. Acacia mangium:
  Diagnostic: Both are glossy black seeds attached to an orange coiled aril. A. auriculiformis is larger (5-7 mm) with an aril completely encircling the seed. A. mangium is tiny (3-5 mm) with the aril folded only at the base.

* Senna obtusifolia vs. Senna tora:
  Diagnostic: Both are angular, glossy brown cylindrical seeds. S. obtusifolia has distinctly oblique/diagonally truncated ends. S. tora is more quadrangular/rhombic in cross-section.

* Crotalaria juncea vs. Crotalaria retusa:
  Diagnostic: Both are classic notched kidney-shaped seeds. C. juncea is dark olive-black/grey and slightly larger (4-6 mm). C. retusa is glowing golden-amber/yellow and slightly smaller (4-5 mm).

* Leucaena leucocephala vs. Albizia lebbekoides / A. procera:
  Diagnostic: All are flat, compressed brown discs with pleurograms. Leucaena is shiny teardrop/oval with a rich chestnut gloss. A. procera is paper-thin and olive-greenish. A. lebbekoides is pale tan/brown and broadly circular.

* Sindora leiocarpa vs. Sindora brugemanii vs. Sindora velutina:
  Diagnostic: All are massive flat woody dark discs (18-28 mm). S. velutina is readily identified by its thick, bright yellow-orange waxy aril cushion at the base. S. brugemanii has a smaller dark collar. S. leiocarpa is smooth and bare.
